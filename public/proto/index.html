Eight ways to hold the same generator

Every page below runs the real counterpoint engine from dist/ and draws notation in the browser. Nothing goes to Hacklily. Pick the one that feels right, or the parts of several.

  1. 7
    The writing room

    The sheet, with a live editor. Select any phrase in the margin, change its settings, reroll it, or move it into place. Sampled piano, highlighted notes, and complete staves in the PDF.

    New synthesis. White paper in either theme. Keyboard: space, R, N.

  2. 8
    The practice room

    Choose a lesson, then explore an endless feed. Edit the sentence, combine filters, and keep your favorite phrases. Open the collection as a sheet when you are ready.

    New synthesis. Count-in, voices, looping, 2/4/8/12 bars, and a choice of meter.

  3. 1
    The sheet

    The page is a piece of engraved paper. A tray at the bottom adds the next phrase; the margin lists what is on the page with reroll and remove. Title and composer are typed straight onto the sheet.

    Verovio engraving, direct PDF download, MIDI, playback with highlight.

  4. 2
    Phrase deck

    One card per phrase. Every setting is a single tap on a pill, and the card re-engraves the moment you tap. Drag to reorder, duplicate, reroll by seed.

    VexFlow, fastest to load, PDF through the print dialog.

  5. 3
    Practice bench

    Built for a laptop on the piano stand. One exercise at a time, large, with count-in, tempo, hands, loop, and a Next button that writes the next exercise. Finished exercises collect into a printable session.

    abcjs with sampled piano playback and a moving cursor. Keyboard: space, N, R.

  6. 4
    Say it

    A sentence with blanks: “Write four bars of first species counterpoint in D minor, in 3/4 time.” Change a word and the music below changes. “And then…” adds another line.

    VexFlow. Fewest controls of the six.

  7. 5
    Workbook

    An open book. The left page has lessons (warm-up, species ladder, around the keys) and an editable table of contents; the right page is real paginated pages you can turn and print as a booklet.

    Verovio with page layout and the Leland font. Direct PDF download.

  8. 6
    The roll

    An endless feed of phrases matching your filters. Keep the ones you like; skip the rest. Kept phrases become one printable piece.

    abcjs. Keyboard: J, K, Enter, space.

What renders the notes

There is no usable LilyPond in the browser. The only WebAssembly attempt runs a whole Linux container and takes about a minute per render. So the prototypes draw notation from the generator's own note data with one of three libraries, and LilyPond stays as a download for anyone who wants the source.

LibraryInputSizeGood atWatch out for
VexFlow 5Notes drawn by JavaScript calls1.1 MB plus two fontsInstant, per-phrase rendering; full control of layoutGlyphs are text in a web font, so PDF goes through the print dialog
abcjs 6.7ABC text0.5 MBSmall, built-in piano playback with a cursorPlainer engraving; long scores need manual line breaks
Verovio 6.3MusicXML7 MB once, then cachedClosest to LilyPond quality; real pages, titles, MIDI, timing mapSlow first load; re-engraves the whole score on each change

Shared code lives in public/proto/shared/: a small adapter over the generator, converters to ABC and MusicXML, the three renderers, a Web Audio player, and the PDF helpers. Any page can swap its renderer for another one.

Running them

Build once with bun run build, serve dist/, and open /proto/. Each page keeps its phrases in the URL, so a link reproduces the same score.
